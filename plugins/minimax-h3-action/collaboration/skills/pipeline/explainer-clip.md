# Single Explainer Clip

Design one explanatory clip at the user's exact duration, not a complete explainer video or editing plan. Communicate one idea through one spoken line or one compact visual explanation.

## Priorities

- Make the promised information visible immediately. Do not spend time on greetings, logos, channel introductions or "in this video" preambles.
- Use one structure: `question/problem -> visible explanation or proof -> resolved understanding`. Every beat advances that same message.
- Treat narration as the timing spine. Preserve exact user narration. If wording is not fixed, write only what comfortably fits the duration, allowing pauses and visual comprehension; never rush technical or numerical language.
- Map every spoken claim to a visible subject, action, change, comparison or result. Remove narration that has no visual counterpart.
- Use a stable visual language. Let the amount of distinct visual information and available reading time determine the shot count; do not apply a numeric cap. Every cut must introduce necessary new information, otherwise prefer one controlled camera move.
- Separate off-screen narration, synchronized effects, ambience and optional music. On-screen people keep their lips still during off-screen narration.
- End immediately after the explanation resolves. Do not invent a CTA unless requested.

## Guardrails

- Do not design subtitles, lower thirds, diagrams requiring exact generated text, editing transitions or a multi-clip sequence. Those belong to external post-production.
- Do not over-promise, introduce a second idea, or use decorative camera movement that competes with comprehension.
- Facts, numbers and product claims must come from the user or supplied material.

Source basis: MiniMax H3 official prompt-writing guidance; YouTube retention guidance on immediate promise delivery; established explainer script-to-screen practice. The one-message and short-runtime limits are gateway synthesis for single-clip generation.
