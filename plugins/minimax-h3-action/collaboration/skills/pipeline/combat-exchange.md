# Combat Exchange Clip

Design a readable combat passage for one generation clip, not a whole fight. Give each participant a distinct immediate objective and make attack, defense, contact and consequence causally connected. Preserve the requested reversals and decisive events when they fit the clip.

- Scale contact density to the requested pace and what remains physically legible. Do not limit longer clips to a fixed number of contacts or stretch sparse action into an unrequested slow-motion display.
- Keep support feet, balance, facing, distance, occupied limbs, weapon ownership and escape routes physically compatible. A limb or weapon cannot perform two conflicting jobs at once.
- Make attacks concrete: body part or weapon, trajectory, target, defensive response and resulting displacement. One shot may contain several linked actions; avoid isolated lists of named moves and one-attack-per-shot coverage that breaks a continuous exchange.
- For sustained combat, every contact immediately causes the next motion: deflection opens a line, recoil powers a counter, a miss creates overextension, and displacement becomes pursuit. Do not end each shot by stepping back to a standard distance, lowering weapons into readiness, standing steady, or holding a static clash. Preserve active momentum across cuts unless the requested drama explicitly includes a pause or standoff.
- Show impact through contact behavior, body displacement, debris, fabric, hair, breath, weapon vibration or environment response. Effects follow the physical action and do not hide the contact point.
- Sustained combat normally uses `continuous`. Use `limited_sequence` only when a cut is needed to reveal a decisive contact, scale change or reversal; preserve screen direction and spatial axis across it.
- When the approved motion contract says `action_continuity: continuous`, treat the exchange as one uninterrupted physical chain even if the camera cuts: no neutral reset, readiness pose, stalemate, pause or static showcase between contacts. A short final reaction or line may be the only intentional hold when the approved ending requires it.
- Preserve user-supplied and approved producer-proposed measurable speed anchors locally. Do not replace subject speed with camera shake, playback speed or generic speed lines. Ordinary fast combat does not require meters, per-action seconds or a numerical anchor; describe the actual collision, deflection, counter and follow-through without delay.
- End on a readable consequence, separation or continuing motion that can be edited into another externally planned clip. Do not invent a complete victory unless requested.

Source synthesis: physical continuity and impact principles distilled from the supplied Seedance combat material; rewritten for short MiniMax H3 generation clips without Seedance-specific templates, fixed characters or multi-shot quotas.
