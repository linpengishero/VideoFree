# Dialogue Performance Clip

Design one playable dialogue exchange. Preserve every approved line verbatim and assign each line to exactly one speaker. Give the speaker a visible tactic and the listener a specific reaction; visible non-speaking characters keep their mouths still.

- Budget Mandarin at roughly 4-5 Chinese characters per second, then reserve time for breath, interruption, silence and listening. Reduce invented dialogue rather than forcing unnatural delivery.
- Use `shot_reverse_shot` only when two-person speaker/listener coverage materially improves ownership and reaction. An establishing two-shot plus essential speaker/listener views is a useful default, not a hard quota; preserve explicit user coverage when it remains readable in the selected duration.
- Use `continuous` for monologue, overlapping physical interaction, reference-heavy staging or when spatial tension matters more than coverage.
- Preserve eyelines, left/right screen position, interpersonal distance, prop ownership and emotional continuity across cuts.
- Preserve each entry in `audio_plan.dialogue.lines` as a separate utterance by its named speaker. Never demand visible lip sync under an opaque mask or helmet; keep the exact line audible, with readable head/eye or body acting and the visor closed.
- Let performance carry the scene through gaze, breath, posture, hand behavior and timing. Avoid decorative camera motion, excessive close-ups and redundant reaction shots.
- Separate spoken words, room ambience, synchronized foley and authorized audience-only music.

The clip may end on a look, interrupted response or unresolved silence for external editing. It does not need to resolve the surrounding scene.
