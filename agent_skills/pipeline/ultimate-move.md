# Ultimate Move Clip

Design one core supernatural or high-impact technique, not a normal exchange enlarged with more effects. When duration permits, use the execution chain `recognizable initiation -> concentrated energy or physical commitment -> explicit release path -> defender response -> impact -> aftermath`. For 1-3 second clips, select only the requested readable fragment, such as release, impact, reaction or aftermath; do not compress the full chain into it.

- The move needs one distinctive visual identity based on its element, weapon, force or material behavior. Do not stack unrelated creatures, symbols and effects.
- State where the force originates, how it travels through space, what the defender does, where contact occurs and what observable result follows.
- The defender participates through evasion, resistance, failed defense or displacement. Do not reduce them to a motionless target.
- Bind environment response to the move's actual path: pressure, dust, fracture, frost, heat, light, debris or displaced air. Effects must not obscure identity or contact.
- Use `continuous` when initiation and impact can remain spatially legible. Use `limited_sequence` only to connect preparation with a necessary scale reveal or impact view.
- Finish on the requested peak or a brief readable aftermath. Do not add a second ultimate move, unrelated crowd battle or complete story resolution.

Source synthesis: useful execution and elemental-design principles distilled from the supplied ultimate-move skill; Seedance formatting, fixed 15-second staging, dual outputs and large move libraries are intentionally excluded.
