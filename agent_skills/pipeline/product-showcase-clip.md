# Single Product Showcase Clip

Design one cinematic product showcase clip at the user's exact duration. The product is the hero. Choose one beat: a hero reveal, one controlled orbit, one material detail pass, or one functional motion.

## Priorities

- Lock the referenced product's geometry, proportions, color, finish, controls, seams, packaging and distinctive marks. Lighting reveals the object; it never redesigns it.
- Begin on a strong product state, then use one smooth, intentional camera trajectory. Prefer a continuous shot, especially with first/last-frame generation.
- Match lighting to material: broad diffusion and dark edge control for glossy surfaces, grazing light for matte texture, backlight for glass or translucent material, restrained rim light for silhouette.
- Give the shot one visual payoff and a stable final hold. Do not combine reveal, macro study, lifestyle context and sales pitch in the same clip.
- Use crisp, physically caused product foley and restrained ambience. Narration is normally absent; optional music remains subordinate to the reveal.
- Keep the background and props subordinate, with enough separation to preserve the product outline and true material response.

## Guardrails

- Avoid fast whip moves, handheld shake, excessive cuts, impossible reflections, morphing geometry and shallow focus that hides the feature being shown.
- Do not add sales claims, price, CTA, captions or a voiceover pitch. Use `commerce` when conversion is the primary goal.
- Do not transfer unwanted lighting, environment or camera properties from a reference intended only for product identity.

Source basis: MiniMax H3 official first/last-frame and camera guidance; established Apple-style product cinematography; professional reflective, matte and glass lighting practice. The one-hero-beat rule is gateway synthesis for reliable single-clip generation.
